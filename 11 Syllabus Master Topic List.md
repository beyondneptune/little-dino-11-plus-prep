# 11+ Grammar School Preparation Syllabus: master topic list

Source: an original syllabus document compiled from the DfE National Curriculum,
publisher familiarisation materials and public school admissions pages. Topic IDs below
(e.g. `MA-C05`) were assigned in order and are STABLE: do not renumber them. Topics
marked "(stretch)" or "(useful extension)" are not core.

**There is no single national 11+ syllabus.** Schools choose their own subjects,
question types and difficulty. Core = foundational for that subject; School-specific =
only some schools; Stretch = harder extensions.

## Exam formats vary by exam family (always confirm from the official admissions page)

Different 11+ exam families test different subject mixes and may or may not include a
writing task. Broadly:

| Exam family | English | Maths | VR | NVR / Spatial | Writing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GL Assessment-style | Yes | Yes | Often | Often | Sometimes (stage 2) |
| CEM / consortium-style | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Sometimes |
| Regional county tests | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Sometimes |
| Bespoke super-selective | Yes | Yes | Varies | Varies | Often (stage 2) |

The exact subjects, question types and difficulty differ by school, so always confirm
from the school's or consortium's own official admissions page.

## Additional preparation for highly selective schools (extensions, not a claim that every topic is examined)

| Area | Additional preparation |
|---|---|
| Advanced English | Longer classic-fiction extracts, complex inference, implied author attitudes, comparing contradictory evidence |
| Advanced vocabulary | Literary, nuanced and less familiar words; precise meaning from subtle context |
| Advanced maths | Reverse percentages, overlapping constraints, complex ratio, non-routine geometric reasoning, systematic counting |
| Written maths | Showing reasoning clearly, organised working and multi-step solutions without multiple-choice options |
| Written English | Planning, drafting and proofreading original narratives and descriptions under strict timing |
| Advanced VR | Multiple simultaneous code rules, more demanding word relationships, compound logical deduction |
| Advanced NVR | Complex 3D manipulation, multiple-rule matrices, mentally combining several transformations |

## Exam technique (a separate strand: `ET-01` to `ET-11`)

- `ET-01` Reading unfamiliar questions accurately, including instructions requiring two answers
- `ET-02` Completing a separate multiple-choice answer sheet
- `ET-03` Allocating time to questions with different difficulty levels
- `ET-04` Recognising when to skip a question and return to it
- `ET-05` Eliminating incorrect answer options logically
- `ET-06` Performing calculations without a calculator
- `ET-07` Managing section-specific timing restrictions
- `ET-08` Producing clear working for written maths examinations
- `ET-09` Completing proofreading within a fixed time
- `ET-10` Managing attention and accuracy over the full test
- `ET-11` Reviewing errors by topic and type, rather than merely looking at overall marks

Example formats vary (verify each): some county tests use two multiple-choice papers of about
45 minutes (one verbal, one maths/non-verbal); others set separate English, maths and reasoning
papers with individually timed reasoning sections.

## Mastery thresholds (internal coaching targets, NOT official pass marks)

| Level | Topic-test performance | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Not secure | Below 60% | Relearn concepts and work through explained questions |
| Developing | 60-74% | Strengthen application and eliminate recurring mistakes |
| Secure | 75-84% | Progress to more demanding mixed questions |
| Strong | 85-89% | Begin harder timed question sets |
| Mastered | 90%+ repeatedly | Test retention through mixed, timed mocks |

A topic counts as mastered only when performance stays strong on previously unseen questions,
including under realistic time pressure.
