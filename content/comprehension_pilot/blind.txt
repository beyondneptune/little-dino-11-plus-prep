=== PASSAGE P1: The Trilobite's Armour ===
Trilobites were small sea animals that lived long before the dinosaurs. Their bodies were covered by a hard shell, a bit like the shell of a woodlouse today. The shell was split into three long parts, which is how the trilobite got its name. When danger came near, a trilobite could roll itself into a tight ball so that only its armour showed. Soft parts, like its legs and feelers, were tucked safely underneath. Because the shell was so hard, it often turned into a fossil after the animal died, while the soft parts rotted away.

=== PASSAGE P2: Maya's Rockpool ===
Maya crouched beside the rockpool, her knees damp from the wet sand. At first the water looked empty, but she waited, as still as the rocks around her. Slowly, a small crab crept out from under a green frond of seaweed. It lifted one claw, as if to warn her, then scuttled sideways across the pool. Maya did not touch it. She had learned on her last visit that grabbing at sea creatures only frightened them away. Instead she smiled and watched until the crab tucked itself back into a crack. When she finally stood up, her legs were stiff, but she did not mind at all.

=== PASSAGE P3: How a Fossil Forms ===
A fossil does not appear overnight. First, an animal dies and sinks to the bottom of a lake or sea. Mud and sand drift down and cover the body, layer upon layer, until the soft parts rot away. The hard parts, such as bones or shells, stay behind. Over many thousands of years, the mud is squeezed into rock, and tiny minerals seep into the bones and turn them to stone. Much later, the rock may be pushed up to the surface, or worn away by wind and rain, until at last the stony fossil is uncovered. Only then can someone find it and learn about the animal that lived so long ago.

=== PASSAGE P4: The Lighthouse Keeper ===
Every evening, old Tom climbed the winding stairs of the lighthouse to light the great lamp. The climb left him out of breath, but he never missed a night. Ships far out at sea depended on his light to steer them safely past the sharp rocks below. One stormy night, the wind howled so fiercely that the lamp flickered and nearly went out. Tom cupped his hands around the flame and would not let it die. By morning the storm had passed, and in the harbour the fishing boats were all safely tied up. Tom smiled, rubbed his aching legs, and went down to sleep at last.
